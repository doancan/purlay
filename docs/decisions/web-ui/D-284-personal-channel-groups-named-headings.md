# D-284. A person groups their own channels under named headings across devices — #1198; amends D-195 and preserves D-132

**Context.** A person working across sales, finance, warehousing and other departments needs to scan a long channel list by their own work. The fixed sections and global filters of D-195 help reduce the list but cannot name those groups. These are personal navigation, never teams, shared categories, permissions or another business workflow.

**Decision.**
1. **Named personal headings.** A workspace member creates, renames, folds and deletes their own channel groups from Sidebar options and each heading's menu. A joined public or private channel moves through its existing row menu. Conversations and People keep their roles. Deleting a heading returns its channels to Channels, preserving every message and membership.
2. **One visible placement.** A channel is in Starred, one custom group, or Channels. Moving it clears its star; starring clears the custom placement. Unstarring does not resurrect a former group. A transaction locks the membership before either write. Client writes on the same channel share the star's ordered line, and only the last refreshes authoritative state. Pending intentions repaint over the last server-confirmed state; refused changes remove their own intention, so multiple refusals cannot restore an unconfirmed predecessor. Queued requests and cache writes are bound to the original signed-in person and do not cross an account change.
3. **The existing disclosure rules.** Each group uses the existing section heading and channel rows. Folding preserves unread, mentions, open incidents and the current channel. Global ordering, unread-only and muted filters still apply. Empty headings explain how to move channels into them. Unknown or failed group data leaves channels reachable in Channels, with recovery. Reset sidebar resets D-195's existing local display options; it does not delete named groups.
4. **Kept by the server, only for the owner.** Chat owns `channel_groups` and `channel_group_channels` (chat/0011); no core or legacy table gains a field. Groups belong to the caller's workspace membership. Placement belongs to the channel membership and a group of that same user, and the service checks the workspace too. Membership removal cascades; rejoining does not resurrect placement. `CHANNEL_GROUP_LIMIT` and `CHANNEL_GROUP_NAME_MAX` in `@purlay/types` own the limits. Names are trimmed, NFC-normalized and unique without case within this owner's workspace. The owner membership serializes creation and rename, including the cap; client-generated ids make creation retryable.
5. **Bounded contracts and private synchronization.** Group metadata is keyset-paged. The existing per-caller channel response carries `sidebarGroupId`, projected in one join; nobody else's placement appears. An unjoined visitor or DM has no placement. Only the owner's `CHANNELS_UPDATED` announces a change, carrying the workspace and no names or ids. Open/reconnect reads these private queries again. Forms and menus keep the current components, deferred chunk, keyboard/focus behavior and EN/TR copy. The move dialog outlives a row changing section and closes when its channel is revoked.
6. **Personal data and lifecycle.** The own personal export includes headings and placements; a workspace export omits both. The generated normalized name key is omitted as derived. Account erasure removes both, including when chat is stopped. Optional-table handling preserves exports from older stopped chat schemas. Workspace deletion removes headings; channel deletion removes placement. The ordinary local display options of D-195 remain local.

**Rejected.** Shared groups or permission changes: a different product capability. Drag-only placement or per-group sort/filter controls: the existing menu and global controls complete the job with a keyboard and on touch. A channel in several groups: it breaks D-132's one-row rule. Remembering a hidden group behind Starred: unstarring would move a row to a place the person did not choose. Keeping these new names and channel ids in browser storage: it would not follow the person to their other devices.

**Cost of reversing.** Two private chat-owned tables and additive contracts. Their rows can be removed without changing channels, messages or membership; the core continues to run with chat absent.
